Hex 7×4

A flat-top honeycomb with 26 letters, a symbols key and a period key. Delete, space, shift and return are swipes, so every key can stay large.

Tuning

Typewise-fit keeps each letter near its place on the Typewise keyboard. Thumb-split moves 6 letters, so every vowel sits on the right and the common consonants on the left. Keep e·a leaves e and a where Typewise has them and puts every vowel in the second and third columns from the left, which moves 14 letters. Any 26-letter order works.

Event log

    How the layers were laid out

    LettersEach letter takes the 7×4 slot nearest its place on the Typewise keyboard, matched one to one. Rows drop from five to four and the two space keys are gone, so letters moved 0.5 key widths on average, and 25 of 26 moved less than 0.75. Finger travel across common letter pairs stays level with the original: 2.61 key widths against 2.62 (rough figure from the 42 most common English pairs). Thumb-split and Keep e·a come from a model of two-thumb typing run on published English letter-pair counts, where the model scores them about 19% and 23% faster than the Typewise-fit order. The Thumb-split order also scores 17% to 23% faster in Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese and Dutch; Keep e·a was only checked on English. The model's constants are assumed and no one has tested these orders yet, so treat them as candidates to try.

    NumbersThe three middle columns follow the phone keypad: 1 2 3 on top, * 0 # on the bottom. Symbols sit at the sides: quotes and brackets on the left, price and math signs on the right, with the hyphen and apostrophe beside the digits. The period key stays put, and holding it still gives , ? ! '.
